**Wall 1 has a positive geometric answer at the local level.** There is a fixed pair of CM elliptic curves over \(\mathbb Q\) and a fixed formal map between them whose \(N\)-jet has denominator
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
d_N=\prod_{\substack{p\nmid 6\\ \operatorname{Frob}_p\text{ is a three-cycle}}}
p^{v_p(N!)}.}
\]
This holds for every \(N\), away from 2 and 3. At \(p=7\) the first denominator is visible in the coefficient \(-3/7\). At \(p=31\) the entire formal map is integral. Both primes are \(1\bmod 3\) and inert in the auxiliary field \(\mathbb Q(i)\).

There is also a construction **on the supersingular auxiliary CM curve itself**. Its first distinguishing jet occurs at degree \(p^2\). These two constructions pinpoint what the three-conjugate-points argument lost and what a passive level twist cannot recover.

The global calculation gives two further results. First, Wall 1 is equivalent, under the supplied Dirichlet black boxes, to a square-root error in the three-cycle part of the central binomial coefficient. Second, I obtain an **unconditional two-sided square-root oscillation** for that error and for the weighted count of three-cycle primes, with explicit lower constants. The missing estimate is the upper bound. I obtain no constant-width zero-free strip for \(\zeta_F\).

## 1. A proper CM construction that distinguishes the primes at degree \(p\)

Take
\[
E_0:y^2=x^3-2,\qquad E_1:y^2=x^3-8,\qquad u=\sqrt[3]{2}.
\]
Both curves have CM by \(K_0\). Over \(F\) there is an isomorphism
\[
\phi_u:E_0\longrightarrow E_1,\qquad (x,y)\longmapsto(u^2x,u^3y).
\]
In the standard parameter \(-x/y\), its tangent multiplier is \(u^{-1}\).

Let \(\ell_i\) be the formal logarithms with linear coefficient 1. Define the **single rational formal homomorphism**
\[
h=\ell_1^{-1}\circ\ell_0\in\mathbb Q[[T]].
\]
Its tangent multiplier is 1. Over \(F\) it has the alternative description
\[
h=\phi_u\circ [u]_{\widehat E_0},\qquad
[u]_{\widehat E_0}(T)=\ell_0^{-1}\bigl(u\ell_0(T)\bigr).
\]
Here \([u]\) is a characteristic-zero **formal** endomorphism. The algebraic CM endomorphism fields of the curves are still \(K_0\). The rational normalization \(h'(0)=1\) fixes the comparison independently of the choice of a conjugate of \(u\).

### The first derivative test

Write \(E_b:y^2=x^3+b\), and let \(D_b\) be dual to \(dx/(2y)\). For \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), the Hasse invariant in this normalization is
\[
H_p(b)=[x^{p-1}](x^3+b)^{(p-1)/2}
=\binom{(p-1)/2}{(p-1)/3}b^{(p-1)/6}\pmod p.
\]
Thus \(D_b^{[p]}=H_p(b)D_b\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and
\[
\frac{H_p(-8)}{H_p(-2)}=2^{(p-1)/3}\pmod p.
\]
On \(E_0\times E_1\), the diagonal tangent line generated by \((D_{-2},D_{-8})\) is closed under the restricted \(p\)-power precisely when this ratio is 1. For \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), that is precisely the identity Frobenius class. This uses the ordinary Hasse invariants of two fixed curves. [Harvey–Sutherland, Hasse–Witt coefficient formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3246)

| \(p\) | Roots of \(t^3-2\) in \(\mathbb F_p\) | \(H_p(-2)\) | \(H_p(-8)\) | Diagonal line \(p\)-closed |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(7=4(0)^2+2(0)(1)+7(1)^2\) | none | 1 | 4 | no |
| \(31=2^2+27(1)^2\) | \(4,7,20\) | 4 | 4 | yes |

There is a characteristic-zero check. Use the integral local parameter away from 2 given by \(x=z^{-2}\), with the branch \(y=-z^{-3}\sqrt{1+bz^6}\). Then
\[
\ell_b(z)=\int_0^z(1+bv^6)^{-1/2}\,dv,\qquad
h(z)=z-\frac37z^7+O(z^{13}).
\]
The discrepancy between the two Hasse invariants has become an actual denominator of a rational Taylor coefficient.

### The exact denominator of every jet

Let \(J_N(h)\) be the linear map on truncated local rings induced by substitution \(g\mapsto g\circ h\bmod T^{N+1}\). Define \(d_N\) as its least common denominator over \(\mathbb Z[1/6]\), chosen prime to 6. Using the **whole substitution map** makes this definition invariant under integral changes of parameter on either curve.

**Theorem.** For every \(p\nmid6\),
\[
v_p(d_N)=
\begin{cases}
v_p(N!),&\operatorname{Frob}_p\text{ is a three-cycle},\\
0,&\operatorname{Frob}_p\text{ is an identity or a transposition}.
\end{cases}
\]

**Proof.** In the identity and transposition cases \(t^3-2\) has a simple root in \(\mathbb F_p\). Choose its lift \(u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\). The isomorphism \(\phi_u\) is integral, and every \(u\in\mathbb Z_p\) acts integrally on the elliptic formal group by \(p\)-adic interpolation of integer multiplication. Hence \(h\) is integral to every order. This includes the supersingular primes \(p\equiv2\bmod3\).

In the three-cycle case, \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), \(E_0\) is ordinary, and the residue of \(u\) lies in \(\mathbb F_{p^3}\setminus\mathbb F_p\). Over the completed maximal unramified extension, the height-one formal group is integrally isomorphic to \(\widehat{\mathbb G}_m\). In this coordinate the scalar endomorphism is
\[
T\longmapsto(1+T)^u-1.
\]
Its \(T^N\)-coefficient has valuation **exactly** \(-v_p(N!)\), since all \(u-j\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), are units. Conversely, the coefficient of \(T^n\) in its \(k\)-th power is an integral linear combination of \(\binom{ju}{n}\), \(0\le j\le k\), so has valuation at least \(-v_p(n!)\). Integral coordinate changes act invertibly on the truncated local rings. This proves both inequalities and the formula. [Lubin–Tate, *Formal moduli for one-parameter formal Lie groups*](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/bsmf.1633/)

I also checked the rational substitution matrices directly. They give \(d_7=7\), \(d_{13}=91\), \(d_{14}=637\), and \(d_{19}=12103\). There is an important distinction for a Bost argument here. In monomial coordinates \(J_N(h)\) is triangular with every diagonal entry 1. **Its determinant is identically 1. Its operator denominator carries the entire cubic splitting signal.** Passing immediately to the full determinant would discard it.

## 2. What survives on the auxiliary supersingular curve

Take \(A:y^2=x^3-x\), with CM by \(\mathbb Q(i)\), and put \(\alpha=t\) on \(Y\). On the formal completion of \(A_Y\) consider
\[
H_\alpha(T)=\ell_A^{-1}\bigl(\alpha\ell_A(T)\bigr).
\]
This uses one auxiliary CM curve and the tautological section of \(\mathcal O_Y\). Its graph can equivalently be taken in \(Y\times A^2\).

Let \(p>3\) be inert in \(\mathbb Q(i)\), put \(q=p^2\), and work at a place of \(F\) above \(p\). Write \(H_\alpha=\sum c_nT^n\), and let \(a_q\) be the coefficient of \(T^q\) in \([p]_A(T)\). The formal group has height two. Therefore \(a_q\) is a unit and all coefficients of degrees \(2,\ldots,q-1\) in \([p]_A\) are divisible by \(p\). Comparing coefficients in
\[
H_\alpha\circ[p]_A=[p]_A\circ H_\alpha
\]
gives
\[
c_n\ \text{integral for }n<q,\qquad
p c_q\equiv a_q(\alpha-\alpha^q)\pmod p.
\]
Indeed, at degree \(n\ge2\) the multiplier of the unknown \(c_n\) is \(p-p^n\). Before degree \(q\) the remaining numerator is divisible by \(p\). At degree \(q\) its two surviving terms modulo \(p\) are \(a_q\alpha\) and \(-a_q\alpha^q\).

Consequently, at a three-cycle prime the **first nonintegral coefficient has degree exactly \(p^2\)** and valuation \(-1\). At an identity prime all coefficients are integral since each local \(\alpha\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z_p\). In particular \(7\) first appears at degree 49 on this auxiliary curve, while \(31\) never appears. The scalar invariant derivation satisfies \(D^{[p]}=0\) at both primes.

This gives a precise answer about the earlier cancellation. Conjugating a theta value and its evaluation point together preserves the valuation. Here the congruence compares the **fixed tangent normalization** with its \(p^2\)-power inside the commutation law for \([p]_A\). The resulting \(\alpha-\alpha^{p^2}\) can have different valuations on the two Frobenius classes. The cost is also precise. Jets of degree \(N\) on this supersingular curve only detect these primes for \(p\le\sqrt N\). The ordinary pair in §1 detects them for \(p\le N\).

### The sharp obstruction for passive twists

Let \(V\) be a finite free unramified Artin lattice and \(J:M\to M'\) an integral local jet map between free lattices. For the external twist \(J\otimes1_V\), each Smith elementary divisor of \(J\) occurs exactly \(\operatorname{rk}V\) times. Inserting an invertible integral Frobenius matrix on the \(V\)-factor gives the same answer. This follows by tensoring a Smith normal form. For a finite étale pullback, derivations extend uniquely, and \((D\otimes1)^p=D^p\otimes1\). Similarly, tensoring a crystal with a finite-image unit-root factor preserves its Newton slopes, with multiplicities. At \(p>3\) a saturated filtration of an \(S_3\)-lattice with free quotients splits equivariantly by averaging a splitting over \(S_3\).

These statements cover passive level labels, external coefficient twists, and changes of integral frame. They leave room for Frobenius **comparisons**. For example, on the standard rank-two lattice the determinant of \((1+p)I-\rho(g)\) is respectively
\[
p^2,\qquad p(p+2),\qquad p^2+3p+3
\]
for identity, transposition, and three-cycle. This already distinguishes the classes locally. The formal map in §1 goes further by realizing the distinction in one rational jet map for all primes simultaneously.

The repository’s actual prime input uses \(\operatorname{partner}(t,i\,\mathrm{Frob}_p)\) in the abelian host group. Changing \(K_2\) can select different global automorphic representations. Keeping this particular jet map and its partner rule while attaching a level label leaves its elementary divisors as above. A proof using §1 would have to estimate a different map. ## 3. The global estimate and an unconditional oscillation theorem

Let \(C\) be the set of good three-cycle primes and define
\[
B_n=\frac{d_{2n}}{d_n^2}
=\prod_{p\in C}p^{v_p\binom{2n}{n}},\qquad
E_n=\log B_n-\frac13\log\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
Thus \(B_n\) is an ordinary integer divisor of the central binomial coefficient. The ratio removes the unknown linear drift one would encounter in estimating \(\log d_n\) alone.

**Zero-free criterion.** Grant RH for \(\zeta\) and GRH for \(L(s,\chi_{-3})\), as in the question. For every \(1/2\le\theta<1\), the following are equivalent.

1. \(L(s,f_{\chi_3})\) has no zero in \(\Re s>\theta\).
2. \(E_n=O_\varepsilon(n^{\theta+\varepsilon})\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).
3. \(E_n\le C_\varepsilon n^{\theta+\varepsilon}\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\) and all \(n\).

The lower one-sided bound alone is equivalent as well. At \(\theta=1/2\) this is Wall 1. At a fixed \(\theta<1\) it gives exactly the constant-width strip requested in the question.

Here is the analytic calculation, including the prime powers. Put \(P_C(s)=\prod_{p\in C}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\), \(L_f=L(s,f_{\chi_3})\), and delete the factors at 2 and 3 from \(\zeta_{K_0}^{(6)}\). Direct calculation in the three conjugacy classes gives
\[
\frac{\zeta_{K_0}^{(6)}(s)}{L_f(s)}
=\frac{P_C(s)^3}{P_C(3s)}.
\]
The identity and transposition local ratios are 1. At a three-cycle the ratio is \((1-p^{-3s})/(1-p^{-s})^3\). In particular, the first correction is at **prime cubes**. This matters at the square-root boundary.

Set \(c(p)=3\mathbf1_C(p)-1\) for every prime and
\[
H(s)=\sum_p\frac{c(p)\log p}{p^s-1}.
\]
Logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
H(s)=\frac{L_f'}{L_f}(s)-\frac{L'}L(s,\chi_{-3})+U(s),
\qquad U\ \text{holomorphic on }\Re s>1/3.
\]
Explicitly, \(U(s)=3\sum_{p\in C}\log p/(p^{3s}-1)-b'(s)/b(s)\), where \(b(s)=(1-2^{-2s})(1-3^{-s})\).

Now put \(A(x)=\sum_p c(p)v_p(\lfloor x\rfloor!)\log p\), taking \(A(x)=0\) for \(x<1\). Its dyadic difference has Mellin transform
\[
\int_0^\infty \frac{A(2x)-2A(x)}3\,x^{-s-1}\,dx
=\frac{(2^s-2)\zeta(s)H(s)}{3s},\qquad \Re s>1.
\]
At integers the integrand’s numerator divided by 3 is \(E_n\). Between successive integers the discrepancy is \(O(\log(2+x))\). The factor \(2^s-2\) cancels the pole at 1.

For the forward implication, Perron inversion for \(A(x)\) gives \(H(1)x+O_\varepsilon(x^{\theta+\varepsilon})\). Indeed its Dirichlet coefficients are bounded by \(2\log n\), the logarithmic derivatives are \(O_\varepsilon(\log^2(2+|t|))\) a fixed distance to the right of the hypothesized zeros, and RH supplies the Lindelöf bound for the factor \(\zeta\) there. The dyadic difference cancels \(H(1)x\). Conversely, the displayed transform has no real singularity to the right of \(\theta\), by positivity of the eta Mellin integral and the supplied Dirichlet theorem. Landau’s theorem applied after adding a one-sided majorant excludes complex poles there. An off-line zero of \(L_f\) would supply such a pole, since \(\zeta\) and \(L(s,\chi_{-3})\) are nonzero there. This proves the criterion. [Eta positivity and the one-sided Landau argument](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/frontier_a_report.md:191)

### A lower bound the global method must respect

Write \(\vartheta_C(x)=\sum_{p\le x,\ p\in C}\log p\) and \(\vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\). I get the following **without any RH hypothesis**:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\vartheta_C(x)-\vartheta(x)/3}{\sqrt x}
&>0.11,&
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\vartheta_C(x)-\vartheta(x)/3}{\sqrt x}
&<-0.11,\\
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{E_n}{\sqrt n}
&>0.03,&
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{E_n}{\sqrt n}
&<-0.03.
\end{aligned}
\]
The general phenomenon of oscillation for Chebotarev sets has a literature. The field-specific conclusions here are the explicit two-sided constants and their transfer to the denominators of the elliptic jets. These are lower bounds on fluctuations. [Kisilevsky–Rubinstein, *Chebotarev sets*](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/2015.Kisilevsky.pdf)

**Verification of the zero used in the proof.** There is a zero \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\) of \(L_f\) with
\[
184917/65536<\gamma<184918/65536.
\]
I checked the signs independently with 128-bit ball arithmetic. For \(c=2\pi/\sqrt{108}\), split the completed Mellin integral at its Fricke symmetry point and evaluate
\[
2\Re\sum_{n\le80}a_n(cn)^{-s}\Gamma(s,cn).
\]
At the left endpoint its value lies in \((5.19,5.21)\cdot10^{-7}\), and at the right in \((-1.21,-1.19)\cdot10^{-6}\). The coefficients come by multiplying the eta product through degree 80. Since \(|a_n|\le d(n)\le n\), the omitted tail is bounded by \(2\sum_{n>80}e^{-cn}/c<10^{-19}\). On the entire interval, ball evaluation gives \(\Re\zeta(1/2+it)>0.518\) and \(\Re L(1/2+it,\chi_{-3})>1.038\), using \(3^{-s}(\zeta(s,1/3)-\zeta(s,2/3))\) for the latter. Thus neither factor cancels the pole under consideration. The database ordinate is \(2.8216141092\ldots\). [LMFDB, conductor-108 \(L\)-function and zeros](https://www.lmfdb.org/L/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/108/1/c/a) · [FLINT ball arithmetic](https://flintlib.org/doc/index_arb.html)

**Why this gives both signs.** If the zero has multiplicity \(m\ge1\), the dyadic Mellin transform has residue
\[
r=\frac{m(2^\rho-2)\zeta(\rho)}{3\rho}.
\]
It is regular at the real point \(1/2\). For the prime sum, removing the repeated powers from \(H\) gives
\[
\sum_p c(p)\log p\,p^{-s}
=H(s)-H(2s)-\sum_p\sum_{\substack{k\ge3\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
 c(p)\log p\,p^{-ks}.
\]
The last sum converges absolutely near \(\rho\) and \(1/2\). The term \(H(2s)\) is regular at both points by classical nonvanishing on \(\Re s=1\). Thus the Mellin transform of \(\vartheta_C-\vartheta/3\) is regular at \(1/2\) and has residue \(m/(3\rho)\) at \(\rho\).

For either discrepancy \(D(x)\), an eventual upper bound \(D(x)\le a\sqrt x\) makes \(a\sqrt x-D(x)\) eventually nonnegative. Landau’s theorem and the absence of real singularities for \(s>1/2\) give convergence there. Compare the absolute value of its transform at \(\sigma+i\gamma\) with its transform at \(\sigma\), multiply by \(\sigma-1/2\), and let \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\). This gives \(a\ge|\operatorname{res}_\rho|\). Applying the same argument to \(-D\) gives the other sign. The certified interval, together with \(|2^\rho-2|\ge2-\sqrt2\), gives the constants above.

In particular a Bost estimate forcing \(E_n\) eventually to have one sign, or forcing \(E_n=o(\sqrt n)\), would be false. A one-sided \(O_\varepsilon(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) estimate has exactly the right strength.

## 4. What can actually run over \(K_0\), and what the orthogonal group sees

**The rank-one automorphic source works.** Let \(T=U(1)_{K_0/\mathbb Q}\). Since \(\chi_3^c=\chi_3^{-1}\) and \(\chi_3\) has odd order, it is trivial on the rational ideles. Hilbert 90 therefore gives a character of the norm-one torus by
\[
\widetilde\chi_3(z/\bar z)=\chi_3(z).
\]
It has trivial infinity type. This realizes the cubic character on a torus compact at infinity, with an integral lattice of finite-level functions. Its binary theta series is the given weight-one form. Thus the first obstacle occurs **after** constructing the torus character and its theta series.

### An explicit affirmative answer on \(O(2,2)\)

The split orthogonal Shimura surface, in its product-of-modular-curves realization, already sees \(b_f(p)\). Take the three complex CM elliptic-curve classes of the order of discriminant \(-108\). The reduced forms are \([1,0,27]\) and \([4,\pm2,7]\), and the class group is cyclic of order three. Let \(S\) cyclically permute the three classes. Restrict the degree-\(p\) Hecke correspondence to **horizontal isogenies**, meaning source and target have that same CM order. Counting cyclic kernels gives the following matrices for \(p\nmid6\):
\[
M_p=
\begin{cases}
2I,&p\text{ splits and the prime ideals are principal},\\
S+S^{-1},&p\text{ splits and the prime ideals are nonprincipal},\\
0,&p\text{ is inert in }K_0.
\end{cases}
\]
Indeed the two split-prime isogenies act by the two inverse ideal classes. On either nontrivial class-character line the eigenvalue is respectively \(2,-1,0\), exactly \(b_f(p)\). In particular \(M_{31}=2I\) and \(M_7=S+S^{-1}\). This is a characteristic-zero Hecke correspondence with an explicit finite matrix, including the sign. [Sutherland, horizontal isogenies and ideal-class action](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5370)

The Borcherds construction has a different weight constraint. In signature \((2,n)\) its direct input has weight \(1-n/2\) and the Heegner divisor is prescribed by the **negative-index** coefficients. The divisor generating series has weight \(1+n/2\). For \(n>0\) neither weight is 1, and the holomorphic cusp form \(\eta(6z)\eta(18z)\) has empty principal part. Weight-one binary theta series do enter CM restrictions and seesaw formulas. Ehlen’s weight-one harmonic Maass coefficients and Schofer’s CM-value formulas are instances, with specific pairings and arithmetic outputs. The horizontal matrix above answers the coefficient question directly. The remaining problem is an estimate across \(p\) after projection to the nontrivial character, where \(S+S^{-1}\) has eigenvalue \(-1\). [Borcherds, Theorem 13.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9609022) · [Howard–Madapusi Pera, integral divisors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.00347) · [Schofer on CM averages](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0603714) · [Ehlen on weight-one CM values](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03662)

\log\frac{d_{2n}}{d_n^2}
\le \frac13\log\binom{2n}{n}+O_\varepsilon(n^{1/2+\varepsilon}).
\]
