# Cluster 4 field notes — the automorphic window

`UpperTheory.Top.VWin` bounds the total size of certain Galois cohomology groups over character fields, simultaneously in the prime and in a range of Tate twists. The automorphic argument enters through **congruences**. A deformation-ring calculation bounds a Selmer length by valuations of differences of Frobenius traces. Automorphy puts the relevant representations in families whose traces can be counted and whose sizes can be bounded at the complex places. In the clearest branch, the proof constructs a single nonzero integer whose prime divisors account for the contributions of an entire character orbit. A separate finite-exception argument then finds an interval of scales on which the required estimates hold together. These are distinct mathematical steps in the Lean proof. ## The quantity in the window

Fix a primitive complex Dirichlet character \(\chi\). That is the content of `PChar`, including its nonzero modulus and a proof of primitivity. For a number field \(E\), a prime \(\ell\), and a positive integer \(j\), the quantity ultimately bounded is
\[
 L_\ell(E,j)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Z_\ell}
    \operatorname{Sha}^{1}\!\left(E,\mathbf Q_\ell/\mathbf Z_\ell(-j)\right)
\]
with the repository’s specified local conditions. More precisely, `LData` takes the finite set of places above \(\ell\), a place of the algebraic closure above each, and a character inducing the cyclotomic character. `qzTw` raises it to \(-j\). `Sha` takes the kernel of localization in degree one, and `shaLen` takes its module length. The definition of `VVle` quantifies over the choices of algebraic closure and local data. This is a Tate-twisted Galois module over the host field. No elliptic curve occurs in this definition. [Primitive characters](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/PrimePairSizeCondition.lean:7) · [Coefficient module](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean:13) · [Local data, twists, and lengths](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean:54) · [Localization maps](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean:132)

`VVle E t B` has two clauses. Every length with \(1\le j<t\) is finite. For **every finite set of primes** \(S\),
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in S}\log\ell\sum_{1\le j<t}L_\ell(E,j)\ \le B.
\]
The bound is independent of \(S\). Since a positive integral length contributes at least \(\log 2\), this also forces finite prime support for fixed data and cutoff. The separate finiteness clause matters in Lean because the sum uses `.toNat` on an extended natural. A numerical estimate on that expression alone would lose the distinction between an infinite length and a zero contribution. [Definition of `VVle`](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean:12)

There are two exponents worth keeping separate. The **page exponent** \(a(x)\) determines the field and satisfies
\[
 x^{2+8\varepsilon}\le 2^{a(x)}\le 2x^{2+8\varepsilon}.
\]
The **twist index** \(j\) runs all the way up to \(\lfloor x^{C_t}\rfloor-1\). The field is specified by its fixing subgroup in a cyclotomic field. An automorphism \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^u\) fixes \(E\) precisely when \(u\equiv\pm1\bmod 2^{a(x)+2}\), \(\chi(u)=1\), and both Jacobi symbols at the auxiliary primes \(q,d\) are \(+1\). Thus one can read the construction as combining the real \(2\)-power cyclotomic layer, the field cut out by \(\chi\), and two quadratic characters. Lean uses the fixing-subgroup formulation, so the conclusion applies to every realization satisfying it. [Field and page definitions](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean:19)

The quantifier order gives the word *window* its meaning. For every \(0<\varepsilon<1/52\) and every admissible page function \(a\), there are \(J\ge1\) and \(X_0\). For every \(X\ge X_0\) and every `WinPair` at scale \(X^{1/J}\), there is a \(Y\) satisfying \(X^{1/J}\le Y\) and \(Y^2\le X\). **Every real \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\)** then satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{VVle}\!\left(E,\lfloor x^{C_t}\rfloor,
                    \sqrt{\exp(x^\varepsilon)}\right)
\]
for each corresponding character field. The pair has \(q\equiv3\bmod8\), \(d\equiv7\bmod16\), prescribed logarithmic size ranges, \(q\mid d+1\), and divisibility of \(d+1\) by all sufficiently small odd primes at that scale. `VWinAt` accepts a qualifying pair. Its downstream consumer supplies the separate pair-existence theorem. **The extra one in 401 pays for a multiplicative change of scale.** `WinPairs` needs to accommodate a constant \(c\ge1\) in the page size. It chooses \(k\ge1\) with \(k^{2+8\varepsilon}=c\), writes \(x=kx'\), and goes sufficiently far out that \(k^{400}\le x'\). The estimate obtained at \(x'\) then covers the consumer’s cutoff at \(x\), since
\[

\]
The proof invokes `Nat.floor_mono`, rewrites `401 = 1 + 400`, and applies precisely this inequality. The budget \(\sqrt{\exp(x^\varepsilon)}\) moves in the favorable direction because \(x'\le x\). ## The four counts

Inside the counting argument, fix an outer primitive character \(\chi\), a host \(h\), and an **inner** character \(\psi\). The host modulus is \(N=\operatorname{lcm}(2^{a+2},\chi.N)\,q\,d\). Membership in `h.X c e` means that \(\psi\) is trivial on the host subgroup and \(\psi(-1)=(-1)^e\). Here \(e\) is the twist index. The measured quantity `Vlen` is the coefficient-ring length of a local-kernel group for the twist by \(\psi^{-1}\chi_{\rm cyc}^{-e}\), with local conditions indexed by \(\ell N\). `Vn` normalizes by the coefficient ramification factor. `VLe B` asserts finiteness and \((\log\ell)\,\mathrm{Vn}\le B\). This normalization lets the proof enlarge the coefficient ring to acquire roots of unity and bring the numerical estimate back. The finite exceptional set \(B_\chi\) contains \(3\) and the odd prime factors of \(\operatorname{ord}(\chi)\,\chi.N\,p_K\,h_*\,p_1\). Here \(h_*\) is the class number of \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-p_K})\). Write \(\psi_P\) for `chiP`, obtained by taking the prime-to-\(\ell\) part and removing its local \(\ell\)-component. The four arguments of `of_four_alt` are as follows. ### Case I — a single integer controls many primes

The first reduction, `OneCharU_s`, is already quantitative. At a suitable Galois element \(\sigma\), let \(T=\Psi(\sigma)+1+\Psi(\sigma)^{-1}\), with the restriction and coefficient maps understood. If all admissible representations avoid this trace, Lean produces a finite family \(X\) of pairwise nonisomorphic three-dimensional representations over the imaginary quadratic field such that
\[
                  \mathrm{Vn}\le 2\sum_{\rho\in X}
                        v_\ell(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)-T).
\]
The proof builds a local deformation ring and model, realizes points as representations, identifies the trace difference with the model’s function, and checks that distinct representatives survive passage to isomorphism classes. Automorphy controls which points enter \(X\). Rigidity controls their infinitesimal deformations. ## How the four estimates become one interval

`LemmaK CK` says that for a totally complex \(L\) containing the requisite roots of unity, \(e\ge1\), and \(n=[L:\mathbf Q]\), the group \(H^2(\mathbf Z_\ell(e+1))\) for the specified global data is finite and
\[
 \log\#H^2\ \le\ \exp\!\big((2en)^{C_K}\log(2|\operatorname{disc}L|)\big).
\]
The exponential is already on the right of a bound for the **logarithm** of the order. Its value is uniformity in the inputs. `KFinal` obtains it using `bOdd`. Following that name reaches a rank bound for the span of particular stable homology classes of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_L)\), bounded by the number of complex places.
