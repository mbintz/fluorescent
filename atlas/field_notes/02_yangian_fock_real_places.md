## 1. From matrix-unit commutators to a Bessel eigenvalue

The input is an arbitrary complex algebra \(A\), possibly noncommutative, and elements \(E_{ab}\) satisfying the \(\mathfrak{gl}_n\) matrix-unit commutator relations. Put \(T_{ab}(u)=u\delta_{ab}+E_{ab}\). For lists of row and column indices \(a,b\) of length \(t\), Lean defines
\[
 Q_{a,b}(u)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
       \prod_{k=0}^{t-1}T_{a_{\sigma(k)},b_k}(u-k).
\]
The product is an ordered `List.prod`. The full determinant uses \(a=b=\mathrm{id}\). The order and the shifts matter independently. Row skew-symmetry follows by reindexing permutations, even without the Lie relations. Column skew-symmetry uses a quadratic identity for adjacent shifted factors and an adjacent-transposition argument. The evaluation-form RTT relation itself takes twelve lines of proof after the theorem header: expand the scalar terms and substitute the matrix-unit commutator. [Definitions and RTT](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/RealPlaceAnalysis/StatementE.lean:9) · [RTT and row skew-symmetry](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/RealPlaceAnalysis/RealPlaceTaylor/QuantumDetHighestWeightHolds.lean:14) · [Column interchange](/home/marcus/GitHub/mbintz/incandescent/lean/src/Formalism/RealPlaceAnalysis/RealPlaceTaylor/ShiftedGeneratorEqIteSmulAdd.lean:164)

Two proofs explain why the determinant travels well. For a highest-weight vector \(v\) of weight \(\nu\), take the largest index \(j\) moved by a nonidentity permutation. Every factor to its right acts diagonally on \(v\). Since \(\sigma(j)<j\), the next factor kills \(v\). Only the identity permutation survives, giving \(\operatorname{qdet}E(u)v=\prod_j(u-j+\nu_j)v\). For centrality, the commutator with a generator reduces to the difference between two minors with a replaced index. Off the diagonal they have a repeated row or column and vanish. On the diagonal they cancel. Thus `QDetCentral` says exactly that the determinant commutes with every \(E_{cd}\), enough to transport the eigenspace along the generated action. ## 4. What “Hermitian curvature” estimates

The Bessel geometry starts with ordinary Hermitian Gram matrices. Pair a flag ending in \(z\) against a translated flag ending in \(gz'\), and let \(D_i\) be the successive leading determinants of the cross-pairing matrix. With \(T_z=-h(z,z)\) on the relevant negative vectors, the discriminant quantity is
\[
 G=|D_{r+1}|^2-T_zT_{z'}|D_r|^2.
\]
Here \(r\) is the **local** flag length. The code proves that \(G\) is a signed determinant of the full Gram matrix and is nonnegative on the unitary group under the positivity assumptions on \(T_z,T_{z'}\). On the big cell, \(D_{i+1}/D_i\) recovers a coordinate \(c_i\), and the last ratio recovers the remaining Hermitian pairing \(\zeta\). `StatementBF` uses these coordinates in a weighted \(L^2\) integral with factors \(\prod_a|c_a|^{2(M_a-2)}(|\zeta|^2-TT')^{k-1}\) on its prescribed support.
